# 0008. Peer benchmark and CHF opportunity

**Date**: 2026-09-06
**Status**: In Progress

## Summary

Once the research pipeline (feature 8) has stored a company's safety KPIs, a background task compares each KPI with its industry peers, ranks the gaps that matter most, and estimates what workplace incidents cost the company per year in CHF, plus what reaching the peer median or the top quarter would save. The peer values come from a curated table seeded from published Swiss and European statistics (Suva, the Federal Statistical Office, Eurostat, the ISO Survey), the cost model is a small set of stored, sourced assumptions, and every result is written as an immutable snapshot with the exact inputs and assumptions it used, so any number on the dashboard can be traced back. The dashboard shows the opportunity first, the top three gaps next and the per KPI positions below, with a "how this is calculated" panel; the first snapshot per company also sends the benchmark ready email. Nothing here calls a model: the benchmark is arithmetic on stored rows.

## Requirements

**User stories**:
- As a client, I want to see where my company stands against companies like mine per KPI so that I know whether my safety figures are good or bad.
- As a client, I want a ranked list of what to fix first so that I can bring three points to my management, not eight numbers.
- As a client, I want the annual cost of incidents in CHF, with a range and every assumption visible, so that I can quote it and defend it.
- As a client, I want to set my industry and headcount when the research did not find them so that the benchmark compares me with the right peers at once.
- As an ops team member, I want a Slack alert when a benchmark computation fails so that a client never wonders why the segment is missing.
- As the owner, I want every peer value and assumption to carry its source and a provisional flag so that I can replace the seed before launch and see what is still provisional.
- As the engineer, I want the benchmark to be a pure function over stored rows so that it runs in a unit test, in fixture mode and in Playwright with no vendor call.

Inputs, peers, position and gap, in this order: (1) **inputs**: per active KPI the `company_kpi_current` row with the highest `period_year` (id, value, year, source, confidence, run id); a KPI with no row gets no `results` entry; FTE equals `companies.employees_count` (heads counted as full time, stated as a fixed assumption line in the disclosure of AC-10); the section from `sectionOfDivision(industry_code)`; the band from `sizeBandOf(employees_count)`; `companyUpdatedAt` from the company row read immediately before computing. (7) **confidence**: per KPI the row's `confidence` (`1` for a `client` row, `null` stays `null`); the snapshot's `confidence` is the minimum over the non null confidences of the rows the cost block used, `null` when the cost is `null` or every input is `null`. ## Decision

**Chosen option**: Option 1: a `benchmark-company` task computes an immutable snapshot from a curated peer table and a stored, sourced assumption set, triggered by the research run and by a client facts edit, with the dashboard reading only the snapshot.

The benchmark is deterministic arithmetic over stored rows, the peer data set is a migration seeded CSV with a provisional flag per row, the cost model is seven stored assumptions, and every result is a traceable snapshot that also gives the benchmark ready email its moment.

## Feature design

**Data model sketch** (the target; the three tables are new and were reserved in spec 0002's map as `benchmarks`; one existing table is touched):

| Entity | Kind | Change | Keys and relations |
|---|---|---|---|
| `benchmarks` | G (**new**) | The columns of AC-1; seeded by the generated migration of AC-2 | pk `id`; fk `kpi_key`; unique `(kpi_key, industry_section, size_band, period_year)`; the unique index serves the lookup |
| `benchmark_assumptions` | G (**new**) | The seven rows of AC-2 | pk `key` |
| `benchmark_snapshots` | T (**new**) | Immutable rows, inserted by the task only; the full tenant contract (both timestamps, the organization index, the audit trigger) plus the write grants revoked | pk `id`; fks `organization_id` (cascade), `company_id` (cascade), `research_run_id` (set null); indexes `(organization_id, created_at desc)`, `(company_id, created_at desc)`; Realtime publication |
| `companies` | T (exists) | No columns; `updateCompanyFacts` writes `industry_code` and `employees_count` through the members update policy | 1:N to `benchmark_snapshots` |
| `company_kpi_current` | view (exists) | Read by the task; row ids copied into `inputs` | input only |

The snapshot blocks (version 1, selected by `model_version` through `SNAPSHOT_SCHEMAS`): `inputs: { fte: number | null, section: string | null, sizeBand, industryCode: string | null, companyUpdatedAt: string, kpis: { key, rowId, value, periodYear, source, confidence, researchRunId }[] }` (one entry per KPI with a row); `results: { key, peer: { rowId, rung, industrySection, sizeBand, periodYear, yearMatch, p25, median, p75, sampleSize, provisional } | null, position | null, gapToMedian | null, gapRelative | null, confidence }[]` (one entry per KPI with a row); `gaps: { rank, key, reason: 'cost' | 'distance' | 'fatality', savingMedianChf: number | null, gapRelative }[]`; `cost: { incidentKpi, incidents, lostDays, lostDaysSource, costPerCase, annual, low, high, atMedian, atTop, savingMedian, savingTop } | null`; `assumptions: { key, value, unit, sourceName, sourceUrl, provisional, effectiveFrom }[]`.

**State transitions**: none. Snapshots are immutable; `benchmarkState` is derived: no snapshot and a trigger moment younger than `BENCHMARK_WAIT_MS` → `calculating`; no snapshot and older → `unavailable`; snapshot with `kpis_compared` 0 → `noData`; snapshot with `cost` null → `ready` with positions and gaps and the missing input named on the card; full snapshot → `ready`.

**Key invariants**:
- A snapshot is never updated or deleted by the app; a new computation is a new row, and the newest row per company by `created_at` is the truth the dashboard shows.
- Every figure on the dashboard and in the email is read from a snapshot; the page never recomputes.
- Every peer row and assumption used by a snapshot is copied into it (`results[].peer`, `assumptions`), so changing the seed never changes a stored result.
- `p25 <= median <= p75` on every peer row; the position rule relies on it.
- A snapshot's `model_version` names the rules and the schema that produced it; a formula change bumps the constant, adds a schema to the map, and never silently changes or blanks old rows.
- The task filters every read and write by the loaded company's `id` and `organization_id`; the payload's `companyId` is used only to load that row.
- Fatalities are never priced, and a fatality above zero always ranks first.
- Money is stored unrounded; rounding happens once, at display.
- `provisional` is a data flag, never a code branch: the model treats provisional rows like any other, and only the label changes.
- A trigger failure of the benchmark task never changes a research run's status; a failing benchmark task is invisible to the client beyond the `unavailable` state, by decision.
- A client facts edit and a research run finishing seconds apart produce two snapshots; the newest wins, and the window in which the research triggered snapshot read the company before the edit is accepted (`inputs.companyUpdatedAt` shows it).

**Security model** (compliance scope: revised Swiss FADP with GDPR readiness, spec 0001; no new personal data):
- Client members read their organization's snapshots and update their company's `industry_code` and `employees_count` (existing members update policy on `companies`); they never write snapshots, peer rows or assumptions, and the write grants on `benchmark_snapshots` are revoked from the app roles so no future policy can open them by accident.
- Assigned experts read snapshots through `expert_assignments`; ops read and write everything; peer rows and assumptions are readable by every signed in user (kind G) and writable by ops or a migration.
- The task uses the service client (allowed in `src/trigger/`) with explicit ids; the recompute script uses the service key and the Trigger.dev secret from the local env file, like the invite script.
- Snapshots hold company figures and row ids, no person's name or address; the email carries a company name and two rounded figures to the organization's own members.
- Audit: every snapshot insert is logged by the tenant table trigger with the service actor; peer rows and assumptions are reference data and not audited (migrations own them).
- Abuse: `updateCompanyFacts` is a member only action that triggers a cheap task; the queue's concurrency limit bounds the worker, and the Vercel Firewall rule of spec 0001 covers the route. No quota is added.

**Configuration required**:
- No new environment variable for the app or the worker. `OPS_ALERT_WEBHOOK_URL` (task, exists) carries the failure alert; the email rail's variables exist from spec 0006.
- The recompute script reads `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL`, `SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY` and `TRIGGER_SECRET_KEY` from `.env.local`, swapped to the target environment's values as `docs/auth.md` describes for the invite script.
- Trigger.dev: the `benchmark` queue registers on deploy (`deploy.yml` tasks job).

## Consequences

**Positive**:
- Every number a client sees is arithmetic over rows they can inspect, with the assumption and the peer row it used copied into the snapshot; "traceable to stored inputs" is a property of the schema, not a promise.
- No vendor call and no model call: the benchmark costs nothing per run, runs in a unit test and in Playwright, and cannot hallucinate a peer.
- The provisional flag makes the seed honest from day one and gives the launch a checkable gate.
- Feature 10 (self assessment) needs only a third caller of the task; feature 12 (ops admin) needs only a read view of two global tables; feature 18 (gap report) can quote a snapshot by id.
- The benchmark ready email finally has its moment, on the existing rail, once per company and member.

**Negative / tradeoffs**:
- The peer data set is only as good as the curation. Published statistics stop at the section level and lag a year or two, four KPIs have no official Swiss figure, and the first seed is provisional by design; until the owner replaces it, the dashboard says so on every card.
- Stored snapshots are derived data (a deliberate exception to "compute at read time"): they are immutable, versioned and copied with their inputs, but a seed replacement needs the recompute script to refresh clients, and old snapshots keep old values on purpose.
- The cost model prices lost time incidents only: absenteeism, near misses and fatalities have no CHF line, and a company whose only incident KPI is TRIFR gets no cost. Each is a stated limit in the disclosure, not a hidden one.
- Heads are counted as full time equivalents; a part time heavy company is overestimated. Stated in the disclosure, revisable as an assumption row later.
- A client edit and a research run finishing within seconds can produce two snapshots in either order; the newest wins and the older stays in history. Accepted rather than serialised.
- The 88 division labels and the 21 section names are about 220 new strings per language to translate and maintain.

**Neutral**:
- Two more global tables and one more tenant table, one generated seed migration per replacement, and the `benchmark` queue.
- `docs/benchmark.md` becomes the fourth runbook beside `docs/auth.md`, `docs/email.md` and `docs/research.md`.
- The `Collapsible` and `QuartileBand` primitives join the gallery and the design inventory.
- `EMAIL_TEMPLATE_NAMES` and `ALERT_KINDS` each grow by one entry; the email and alert registries change by a few lines.
